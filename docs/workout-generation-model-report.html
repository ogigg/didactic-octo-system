01 / Verdict

DeepSeek takes the lead.

The expanded scan changes the recommendation. DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 combines competitive reasoning, native JSON Schema, unusually broad provider coverage, and the lowest qualified production cost in the field.

Recommended production candidateDeepSeek / V4 Flash 0731

The GA model reaches up to 90.0% GPQA Diamond and 77.8% TAU-Bench in OpenRouter's provider tests, supports schema-enforced output, and has 28 routed providers. Reka is the clearest current intersection of speed, uptime, JSON Schema, and ZDR, modeling at $0.00253 per workout—about 28% below a ZDR Luna route—while avoiding the 8-token/second endpoint behind DeepSeek's misleadingly cheap headline floor. 1

DeepSeek: why #1

The 0731 GA revision has a month of operational evidence, schema enforcement, the broadest route pool, strong proxy scores, and an eligible fast ZDR route. Its $3.60 headline cost is not used because that endpoint runs at only 8 tps; faster CoreWeave also lacks the required response-format parameter.

GLM: why not #1 yet

GLM 5.3 Flash may ultimately win. Z.ai reports an Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index of 57 and major gains over GLM 5.2. One day of data, no JSON-schema enforcement, and no current route combining response format, ZDR, and strong throughput make it a pilot—not production.

Qwen: why watch

Qwen3.8 Flash is technically compelling and schema-capable, but the current OpenRouter listing has one 37-tps provider, 5.21-second latency, no listed ZDR endpoint, and almost no independent operational history.

MiniMax: why test

M3 has excellent science-reasoning proxies and broad routing, but OpenRouter's observed structured-output errors range from 0.31% to 25.32% across providers. It is a provider-selection problem as much as a model-selection problem.

Superseded variants

DeepSeek V4 Flash 0423, MiniMax M2/M2.7, older Qwen3.x variants, and GLM 5/5.2 remain available but are excluded from the final bake-off because a newer same-family model now covers their role.

Premium controls

Luna and Claude remain valuable controls. They tell the team whether cheaper open-weight candidates actually sacrifice workout quality; they should not be presumed superior without a blind domain evaluation.

04 / Quality bar

Measure the workout.

Generic intelligence scores are only a proxy. A useful bake-off should grade the artifacts users actually train from. The rubric reflects current ACSM guidance: individualize, prioritize adherence, cover major muscle groups, and match load and volume to the goal.8

35%

Programming quality

Movement balance, exercise order, weekly overlap, goal-specific sets/reps/rest, feasible duration, and sensible variety.

25%

Safety & constraint fidelity

Equipment match, hard-dislike exclusion, conservative new loads, difficulty calibration, fatigue management, and no invented IDs.

20%

Progression continuity

History-aware choices, appropriate progression or regression, stable stimulus, and regeneration feedback that changes the right thing.

10%

Contract reliability

Schema-valid JSON, correct weight/time fields, one warm-up per weight exercise, concise rationale, and zero repair substitutions.

05%

User-facing clarity

Useful exercise notes and rationale without jargon, false precision, unsafe medical language, or hidden-chain-of-thought leakage.

05%

Operations

P95 completion under the app’s 15-second timeout, stable availability, predictable token use, and total cost including retries.

05 / Recommendation

Ship by evidence.

The repository already validates outputs with Zod and deterministically applies progression after generation. That reduces how much intelligence the model must supply and makes a small model the rational first choice.

Run a 160-case offline bake-off

Compare DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731, GLM 5.3 Flash, MiniMax M3, Qwen3.8 Flash, Luna, and Claude across novice/advanced profiles, every split and equipment tier, sparse/rich history, dislikes, regeneration feedback, and adversarial custom prompts. Use at least two qualified coaches, blinded to model identity.

Enforce the schema upstream

Use OpenRouter JSON Schema for DeepSeek, MiniMax, Qwen, Luna, and Claude. GLM 5.3 and DeepSeek V4 Pro only support JSON mode, so measure their Zod failure and substitution rates separately. Continue server-side validation in every lane.9

Route by throughput, privacy, and price

Set require_parameters: true, zdr: true, data_collection: "deny", a preferred minimum throughput, and a maximum price. Default OpenRouter routing favors cheap endpoints; for DeepSeek that can select an 8-tps provider and defeat the app's 15-second timeout.10

Log the outcome, not just the call

Track exact model revision and provider, quantization, token classes, TTFT and end-to-end latency, validation failures, substitutions, fallback/regeneration rate, coach score, user difficulty feedback, and workout completion. A model name alone is not an experiment identifier.

Promote on a fixed gate

Promote the cheapest candidate that lands within 3 percentage points of Claude on blinded coach score, exceeds 99.5% valid outputs after one attempt, respects every hard constraint, stays below 15 seconds at P95, and has an eligible ZDR route. Keep Luna as the control until an open model clears all gates.

06 / Confidence

What we know.

The recommendation is strong on integration, routing, privacy, and economics; moderate on general quality proxies; and still unproven on the actual workout domain.

Published fact

Live OpenRouter catalog data supplied IDs, per-token prices, context, supported parameters, provider inventory, ZDR eligibility, latency, throughput, uptime, and observed errors.

Repository fact

The generator supplies up to 100 catalog exercises, requests strict JSON, validates with Zod, substitutes invalid IDs, and applies deterministic progression afterward.

Analyst judgment

Workout-fit ratings, qualified-provider selection, the 7,000-input/1,500-output token scenario, and the decision that DeepSeek currently has the strongest evidence-to-cost position.

Primary model research

Official Z.ai, DeepSeek, Qwen, and MiniMax release materials were checked for architecture, release status, intended workload, and vendor-reported benchmarks. Vendor scores are labeled as such and are not treated as workout evidence.

Operational screen

The cost ranking rejects headline endpoints that cannot plausibly meet the timeout. The screen requires credible sub-15-second end-to-end evidence or roughly 70+ output tokens/second, near-99% uptime, required parameters, and ZDR; final acceptance still uses app-measured P95.

Market coverage

The longlist included current and superseded models across seven families. Older same-family releases were researched, then removed from the final table when a newer model covered the same price/capability lane.

Important limitation: no API key was available, so the models were not run against identical app prompts and no outputs were coach-scored. GPQA and TAU-Bench test scientific reasoning and tool-use reliability, not exercise prescription. “Best” therefore means the strongest production candidate from current published and operational evidence—not a proven winner for personalized workouts.
